# Viral Patterns Pack

Pre-loaded research. Do **not** go run live competitor research during a workshop session — read this file, match the participant to a lane, and move. Live research is the fallback, not the default.

**Last refreshed:** September 2026
**Sources:** Sandcastles outlier analysis (founder/business/AI watchlist + global scope), vidIQ short-form outlier data, and the AI Empowerment Now hook library.
**Refresh cadence:** every 8–10 weeks. See "Refreshing this file" at the bottom.

---

## How to use this file

1. Read the participant's product/service and pick their **lane** from the Lane Matcher.
2. Pull that lane's **winning structures**, **saturated territory**, and **open lane**.
3. Feed the hook templates into the Copywriter with the participant's own words filled into the brackets.

Every template below is a **madlib**: a proven sentence shape with the specifics removed. The shape is what travels between niches. The specifics are what make it theirs. Never hand a participant a madlib with the brackets still in it.

## The Lane Matcher

Nine lanes. Pick the closest by **what the buyer is actually buying**, not by industry label.

| Lane | They sell | Buyer is buying | Typical participant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 — Physical product, self-care** | A thing you put on or in your body | A result they can see or feel | skincare, supplements, wipes, wellness CPG |
| **L2 — Physical product, other** | A thing you use | A problem going away | home goods, tools, pet, kids, apparel |
| **L3 — Expert service, high ticket** | Your judgment | Risk removal | consulting, legal, financial, agency, fractional exec |
| **L4 — Expert service, local** | Your hands or your time | Convenience plus trust | clinic, salon, contractor, real estate, restaurant |
| **L5 — Coaching / transformation** | A new version of them | Belief that it's possible for them | business coach, health coach, career coach |
| **L6 — Course / info product** | A shortcut | Time saved and confusion removed | cohort, course, membership, template shop |
| **L7 — Software / SaaS / AI tool** | A capability | Leverage, headcount avoided | app, platform, AI tool, automation |
| **L8 — Creator / media brand** | Attention and taste | Being early and being in the know | newsletter, channel, podcast, community |
| **L9 — B2B, considered purchase** | An operational outcome | Defensible decision they can justify upward | enterprise services, manufacturing, logistics |

**Unclear fit?** Ask one question: *"When someone buys this, what stops being a problem for them?"* The answer picks the lane. If it still straddles two, use **L3** — it is the most transferable and the templates degrade gracefully.

## The 11 hook categories

Every winning short-form opener falls into one of these. Ordered by how reliably they work for **business owners selling something**, not for general entertainment.

| # | Category | What it does | Works hardest for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem** | Names the pain in the first breath | L1, L3, L4, L7 |
| 2 | **Contrarian** | States a belief the audience holds, then breaks it | L3, L5, L6, L7 |
| 3 | **Secret reveal** | Opens a door the viewer assumed was closed | L3, L6, L8, L9 |
| 4 | **Case study** | Leads with a real number from a real situation | L3, L5, L9 |
| 5 | **Comparison** | Puts two things side by side and makes you pick | L1, L2, L7 |
| 6 | **List** | Promises a countable payload | L4, L6, L8 |
| 7 | **Question** | Asks the thing they've quietly wondered | L1, L4, L5 |
| 8 | **Authority** | Establishes standing before making a claim | L3, L8, L9 |
| 9 | **Personal experience** | A thing that happened to you, told fast | L5, L8 |
| 10 | **Tutorial** | "Watch me do the thing" | L2, L6, L7 |
| 11 | **Scenario** | Puts them inside a hypothetical | L1, L2, L5 |

## Template bank

Fill every bracket with the participant's own words. Outlier notes reflect observed performance in the source pool.

### 1. Problem

- `If you [undesirable task] and use [solution] to [fix it], your [outcome] will be [dramatic result].` — reliably strong across service and tool lanes.
- `For my [specific niche], pay attention to this. This is your [assignment].` — narrow address beats broad address; the narrower the group named, the better it performs.
- `Hiring [role]? Will pay [price]. Doing [alternative] instead.` — works when the alternative is visibly cheaper or faster.
- `[Number] [negative indicators] I spot every time I [common action].` — the expert-eye pattern; very strong for L4.
- `Struggling with [specific problem]? Here's the fix most [peer group] miss.`
- `Stop doing [common practice]. Do this instead for [specific better result].`

### 2. Contrarian

- `In the next [timeframe], you're going to see [trend].` — prediction opener; among the highest outliers observed in the business pool.
- `Everyone thinks [common belief]. The [people who actually know] do the opposite.`
- `Most people think you need [expensive requirement] to [outcome]. I did it with [humble thing].`
- `The [common thing] is never actually [assumed negative state].`
- `I was wrong about [topic]. [The thing that changed my mind] is why.`
- `Why is everyone [doing action]? And no, [popular alternative] is not [the adjective they assume].`

### 3. Secret reveal

- `No gatekeeping. I'm breaking down exactly how [impressive outcome] works.`
- `What [entity] paid me in [period] was [gross]. I kept [much smaller net].` — number-gap reveals perform far above baseline.
- `[Number] things I would [action] this [timeframe] if I were starting over.`
- `Meet the [archetype] who has been [action] for [timeframe], and almost nobody knows [the gap].`
- `There's a [thing] nobody talks about that [surprising benefit].`

### 4. Case study

- `I sell [product/service]. Last year we did [revenue].` — flat, factual, and consistently strong. No adjectives needed.
- `We are a [business type]. We own [assets/stats]. Here's what we'd change.`
- `[Option A] vs [Option B]: which actually gets you to [goal]?`
- `This exact [method] took [starting point] to [ending point] in [timeframe].`

### 5. Comparison

- `This [item] is [comparison adjective] than [the baseline everyone knows].`
- `Which [category] does [job] better?` — pair with a visible side-by-side or it underperforms.
- `[Visual underdog] vs [visual powerhouse].`
- `I gave [A] and [B] the exact same [input]. The results were not close.`

### 6. List

- `Here are [number] [negative indicators] I see every time I [action].`
- `[Number] things I don't do as a [role].`
- `These are my favorite [subject] in [context].`
- `[Number] [upgrades/changes] that cost almost nothing now and save [big amount] later.`

### 7. Question

- `Can [group] [difficult task] under [constraint]?`
- `Why won't [group] [desired behavior]?`
- `Would you trust [role] with [common negative condition]?`
- `Okay, I'm curious. Did you use [method] for any of this?`
- `If someone wants [result] but says "[common objection]," where do they actually start?`

### 8. Authority

- `I have a [system] that [does the surprising thing].`
- `[Product] can now [high-value action] for you.`
- `After [credential or volume], here's the one pattern they all share.`
- `[Role]: [the promise]. [Time context].`

### 9. Personal experience

- `I just saw that [subject] [event], and [honest emotional reaction].`
- `I [achieved/lost] [significant milestone] [timeframe]. Here's what actually mattered.`
- `For the past [timeframe] I've been in [difficult state].` — relatability can override the usual "don't start with I" rule when the state is widely shared.
- `POV: your [person] still [behavior].`

### 10. Tutorial

- `[Tool], make me a [thing] that looks like [desirable], but actually [twist].`
- `I'm going to show you how to [outcome] from [simple input] in [short time].`
- `If you want to [fit more / do more] in your [thing], use the [named method].`

### 11. Scenario

- `[Desirable situation] and no [essential convenience]. Would you?`
- `Imagine [specific situation]. That's what [product] does.`
- `What if [common practice] is the thing hurting your [outcome]?`

## Format library

A format is two things stacked. Never let a participant confuse them.

**Storytelling structure** — how the words are organized:

| Plain name | What it is | 12-second example |
|---|---|---|
| The countdown | A numbered payload delivered in order | "Here are the 5 things I'd never do as a clinic owner" then counts down |
| Head to head | Two options compared, one wins | Splits screen, runs both, declares a winner |
| Myth buster | States the belief, then breaks it | "Everyone says you need X. Watch this." |
| The breakdown | Takes one real example apart | "This brand made $30M. Here's the exact play." |
| Show me | Someone doing the thing, start to finish | Hands on the product, no talking head |
| The receipt | Leads with a real number and explains it | "This invoice was $40K. I kept $6K." |
| Story with a lesson | A short personal story that lands on a point | "Last year I got rejected 27 times, then…" |
| The challenge | A constraint plus an attempt | "Can I do X in Y minutes?" |

**Visual layout** — how it's shot:

| Plain name | What it is | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| You, talking | Full screen, you to camera | Lowest |
| You plus cutaways | You talking, other footage cut in | Low |
| Product only, no face | Close footage of the thing, voice over | Low |
| Split screen | You on one half, the subject on the other | Medium |
| Screen recording | Your screen, narrated | Low |
| Text on screen driven | Words carry it, footage supports | Low |
| Fully generated | AI-built footage, voiced | Medium |

**Which combinations are working:** the business pool is dominated by *educational* structures (countdown, breakdown, myth buster) shot as *you, talking* or *you plus cutaways*. The **open combinations** across almost every lane are: the receipt shot as product-only, the challenge shot as fully generated, and head-to-head shot as split screen with real footage. Those are where a participant with no audience can look different immediately.

## What's saturated right now

Say this plainly to participants. It saves them three months.

- **"Here are 5 AI tools you need"** — completely saturated in every lane. Dead on arrival without a genuinely new tool or a strong personal result.
- **Generic transformation coaching hooks** ("I went from broke to six figures") — saturated and increasingly distrusted.
- **Talking head with captions and no visual payload** — still works, but it is the default, so it wins only on the strength of the words. No visual advantage.
- **Trend audio with unrelated footage** — reach without buyers. Avoid for anyone selling something.

## Where the open lane usually is

- **Showing the actual work.** Most businesses never film the boring middle. Doing so is instantly differentiated and hard to copy.
- **Real numbers with the unflattering part included.** Gross-versus-net, what it actually cost, what didn't work.
- **A named method.** Giving your process a name ("the 2-1-3 method") makes it quotable and makes you the source.
- **Speaking to one narrow group by name.** "For my [specific role]" outperforms "for business owners" almost every time.

## Refreshing this file

Run before each cohort or every 8–10 weeks:

1. Sandcastles: `top_hooks` and `top_formats` at watchlist scope, 90–120 day lookback, then again at global scope for cross-niche patterns.
2. vidIQ: `vidiq_outliers` and `vidiq_instagram_tiktok_outlier_search` for two or three lanes that the upcoming cohort skews toward.
3. Keep the structure of this file identical. Replace templates that no longer appear; add new madlibs to the matching category. Update "Last refreshed" and the saturation list — that section goes stale fastest.
