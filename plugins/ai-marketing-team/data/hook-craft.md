# The Four Corners — Tam Walsh's hook framework

The craft reference behind the Copywriter and the Producer. Teach it in this language. It is built for a founder who has never made a video and does not want a vocabulary lesson.

---

## Why the first three seconds decide everything

A short-form video is not a video. It is a **door**.

If she scrolls past at second two, the other fifty-eight seconds may as well have been a black screen. Not "less effective." Irrelevant. Nobody saw them.

So the math is brutal and simple: **the first three seconds are worth more than the rest of the video combined.** Which means a founder should spend roughly a third of her total effort on them, and almost nobody does.

The second thing to say out loud: for a business owner, the job of content is not views. It's **trust at scale**. Every person who watches to the end trusts you slightly more than they did. Nobody crosses that bridge without a hook. So the hook is not the fun creative part. It is the prerequisite for the entire business case.

---

## The Four Corners

Most people think "hook" means the words you say. That is one corner out of four. A hook is four things happening at the same time:

| Corner | Plain name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **SEE** | The visual | What is on screen in the first seconds |
| **READ** | The text | The words laid over the top |
| **HEAR** | The spoken line | What you actually say |
| **FEEL** | The sound | Music, silence, or a sound effect |

**Order of power: SEE and READ land first.** A person absorbs a picture and a few words faster than she can process a sentence. Which is why a video with no talking can still work, and a great spoken line over a dead visual usually doesn't.

### The rule that matters: all four say the same thing

This is the whole framework.

If you SAY "this changes how people sleep," and the screen SHOWS a rocket, and the text READS "crazy story," and the music FEELS tense, the viewer's brain spends its three seconds trying to reconcile four signals instead of deciding to stay. She bounces. Not because she wasn't interested. Because she was confused.

**Confusion is the number one killer of good hooks.** Not boring. Confusing.

Aligned example:
- HEAR: "These are called lifesize floor plans. You can walk through your exact home before you build it."
- SEE: people walking through a projected floor plan
- READ: "Lifesize Floor Plans"
- FEEL: calm background music

Four corners, one message. Nothing to reconcile.

Misaligned example:
- HEAR: "This company ships containers into space."
- SEE: stock footage of rockets, then a homemade pipe
- READ: "Space Cannons Are Awesome"
- FEEL: tense sound effects

Every corner is interesting. Together they're a puzzle. The viewer leaves.

## The two tests every hook must pass

### Test 1 — The Three-Second Address

Within three seconds, a viewer must be able to answer: **"Is this for me, and what is it about?"**

Three outcomes. She stays. She bounces because it's genuinely not for her, which is fine. Or she bounces because she couldn't tell, which is the tragedy — that was a customer who never got the chance to opt in.

How to pass it:
- **Say what it's about in the first sentence.** Not the second.
- **Use "you" and "your," not "I" and "my."** "I've been thinking about pricing" makes her decide whether she's like you. "If you're afraid to raise your prices" is already about her. The exception: a personal story so widely shared that "I" IS "you."
- **Name the narrow group.** "For my clinic owners" beats "for business owners" almost every time. Naming a smaller group makes more people feel spoken to, not fewer.
- **Fewest words that stay unmistakable.** If a sentence can be read two ways, rewrite it. Clarity beats cleverness, always.

### Test 2 — The Gap

Curiosity is not a mood. It is a **measurable distance** between what she already believes and what you're about to tell her.

No gap, no curiosity. "Prince Charming rides through the forest" is not interesting. "A dragon lands on him" is. The change of state is the whole thing.

The bigger the gap — on a topic she actually cares about — the harder the hook pulls.

Two ways to build it:

**Stated gap.** Name the common belief, then break it.
> "If you want to save on taxes, stop maxing your 401K. Do this instead."

**Implied gap.** Skip the belief, present the new thing, let her build the comparison herself.
> "The biggest tax move wealthy people make isn't a 401K."

Stated is clearer and safer. Implied reaches wider. Both work. Pick based on how likely confusion is.

**The room test for gap size:** put a hundred people in a room who care about your topic. If ninety-nine believe one thing and you're the one saying the other, that's maximum gap. If fifty already agree with you, the gap is half gone. If they all already know it, you have no hook, you have a reminder.

## Writing a hook from scratch, in order

Never skip step zero. It saves an hour.

**0. What footage do you already have?** A clip on your phone, a product on your desk, a before-and-after. If something good exists, build the hook around it instead of writing a hook and then scrambling for visuals.

**1. Write the spoken line.** Easy mode: take a template from the Viral Patterns Pack that's proven in your lane and fill it with your own specifics. Normal mode: answer two questions and compress them into one or two sentences.
   - What do I have to say so she knows this is about her?
   - What's the gap between what she believes and what I'm about to say?

**2. Pick the visual that makes the words easier to understand.** If there's an obvious literal visual, use it. If not: shoot ten seconds on your phone, generate it, or put yourself on camera — but know that if it's just your face, the text is now carrying the whole load.

**3. Write the text on screen.** Fewest words. Biggest font that isn't obnoxious. High contrast so it survives a bright feed. Near eye level. Not tucked under the caption bar or the interface overlays, and not blocking the thing you want her to look at.

**4. Pick the sound.** Silence is a choice and sometimes the right one. Music sets the emotion. A sound effect can stop a scroll. Just make sure it agrees with the other three corners.

**5. Check it.** Below.

---

## The Final Check

Three passes. Thirty seconds. Do it every time.

1. **Watch it on silent.** Is the topic clear? Is there a gap? (This is how most people will see it.)
2. **Listen with your eyes closed.** Same two questions.
3. **Watch it normally.** Are you actually hooked? If you aren't, she won't be.

Then, one honest question: **are you bored, or are you locked in?** Sounds obvious. It is the most-skipped step in content.

## Common failure modes, and the fix

| What went wrong | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The "me" open** | "Something I've really learned lately is…" | Rewrite in "you." Lead with her problem. |
| **Slow start** | Wasted words before the point. "In my opinion," "So basically," "Hey guys" | Delete the first sentence. It's almost never load-bearing. |
| **Corners disagree** | Words say one thing, footage says another | Change whichever corner is cheaper to change. Usually the visual. |
| **No gap** | True, useful, and already known | Find the part she'd argue with. Lead with that instead. |
| **Text that can't be read** | Small, low contrast, on screen for half a second | Bigger, backed, longer. If she can't read it, it doesn't exist. |
| **Jargon in second one** | A term she's never heard | Replace with a plain word or explain it inside the sentence. |
| **Weak visual undercutting a strong claim** | "I built this incredible thing" over something that looks cheap | Either improve the visual or change the claim to match. Mismatch reads as a lie. |

## Physical products, specifically

Do not explain the product. **Show the outcome, or the emotion of the outcome.** What her face looks like after. What the surface looks like after. What stopped happening. The product is evidence, not the subject.

---

## How to know if a hook worked

Average watch time. Higher than your own baseline means the hook did its job. Views follow, they don't lead.

And the honest part: **nobody can promise a hook will work before it ships.** Write four variants, test them, keep the winner, and reshoot the loser a few weeks later with a better opening. Judgment plus reps. Not a formula.
