# Demo Brand — the escape hatch

Use this when a participant is stuck, arrived without a product, or joined late. She runs the full system on this brand, sees the whole thing work, and rebuilds it with her own product that night. **Nobody sits out.**

Say it like this: *"Let's run it on a sample brand so you see the whole machine. Then you do yours tonight with your own product, and it takes twenty minutes because you'll already know the moves."*

---

## Athlique Reset Wipes

**What it is:** Physician-founded facial and body wipes. Single-use, clinical-grade, fragrance-free. Designed for the moment between a workout and the rest of your day when a shower isn't happening.

**Founder:** Brianna Varis, physician.

**Price:** $24 for a pack of 30. Subscription available.

**Who buys it:** Women 28 to 45 who train in the morning and go straight into work, school pickup, or a meeting. She is not an athlete. She is a person with forty minutes and no shower.

**What she says about the problem:** "I either skip the workout or I show up to everything sweaty. There's no version where I get both."

**What changes:** She stops choosing between working out and being presentable. The workout stops being the thing that blows up her morning.

**The unfair advantage:** A physician built it because she watched patients break out from wiping their faces with whatever was in the gym bag. Clinical formulation, not a scent brand. She can speak to the skin barrier with actual authority.

**What stops the sale:** "Wipes are wipes." Price versus drugstore. Skepticism that anything replaces a shower.

**Lane:** L1 — physical product, self-care.

**Brand feeling:** Clean, clinical, quietly expensive. Not wellness-pastel. Closer to a good dermatologist's office than a spa.

**Suggested palette direction:** deep slate, warm off-white, one saturated accent. Restrained, lots of white space, daylight photography.

# Ready-made rescue files

When a participant is being skipped ahead to the Copywriter, copy these two sections into `marketing-team/positioning.md` and `marketing-team/whitespace.md` first. The Copywriter reads those files and will produce generic output without them.

## → `marketing-team/positioning.md`

```
# Positioning — Athlique Reset Wipes

## The buyer
"Dana." 34, trains at 6am, goes straight into work or school dropoff. Not an athlete.
A person with forty minutes and no shower. Has a real skincare routine she abandons
on training days. Has broken out from gym-bag wipes before and remembers it.

## What she says about the problem
"I either skip the workout or I show up to everything sweaty. There's no version
where I get both."

## What changes
She stops choosing between working out and being presentable. The workout stops
being the thing that blows up her morning.

## Why this, why her
A physician built it because she watched patients break out from wiping their faces
with whatever was in the gym bag. Clinical formulation, not a scent brand. She can
speak to the skin barrier with actual authority, which almost nobody in this
category can honestly claim.

## What stops the sale
1. "Wipes are wipes." No perceived difference from drugstore.
2. Price. $24 for 30 against $6 at the pharmacy.
3. Skepticism that anything meaningfully replaces a shower.

## The one-line claim
The physician-made reset for women who train in the morning and have somewhere to be.
Alternates: "Clinical-grade wipes for the forty minutes between the gym and the rest
of your day." / "Stop choosing between your workout and your face."

## The filter
Would this be worth Dana's time, and would it make her trust us more?
```

## → `marketing-team/whitespace.md`

```
# White Space Map — Athlique Reset Wipes

## Lane
L1 — physical product, self-care. Her buyer is buying a result she can see and feel.

## CROWDED
- "5 products in my gym bag" and get-ready-with-me routines. Completely saturated.
- Pastel wellness aesthetics with soft-focus B-roll and trending audio. Reach without buyers.
- Before-and-after skin transformations. Done to death and increasingly distrusted.

## CONTESTED
- Ingredient explainers. Real players here, but most are influencers reading labels.
  Winnable only with genuine clinical authority, which she has.
- "Dermatologist reacts" formats. Crowded at the top, thin in the middle.

## OPEN
- **Clinical plain-speech, no face, product only.** Reading the actual ingredient
  panel and saying what each thing does to the skin barrier, flatly, no music.
  Nobody in self-care does this because almost nobody can.
- **The receipt format.** What a wipe actually costs to formulate versus what the
  drugstore version costs. The unflattering number included.
- **Showing the boring middle.** Formulation, testing, rejected batches. Everyone
  in this category shows the after.

## Her three buckets
1. "What's actually in it" — clinical plain-speech. OPEN. Her advantage made visible.
2. "The morning math" — the forty-minute problem, dramatized. CONTESTED. Proven demand.
3. "Things I've seen as a physician" — patient-adjacent stories, no identifying detail.
   CONTESTED. Highest trust-per-second of the three.

## Traps
Pure aesthetic content performs on views and does nothing for sales in this lane.
Do not chase it.
```
